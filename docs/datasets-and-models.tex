\section*{How to read this document}

Every figure here was produced by a command that can be re-run against this
repository. Where something could not be established, it says so rather than
being filled in plausibly.

Two numbers appear repeatedly and mean different things:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{Held-out test accuracy} --- measured on images the model never
        trained on, but drawn from the \emph{same source} as its training data.
        This flatters a livestock model, because photographs from one farm
        share a backdrop, a camera and a lighting condition.
  \item \good{Source-held-out accuracy} --- measured on photographs from a
        source the model has never seen at all. This is the number that
        predicts field behaviour, and it is the number we quote.
\end{itemize}

The gap between them is the whole reason this project reports what it reports.
An earlier bovine breed model in this project scored \bad{97.9\%} on a
held-out test set and was found to be classifying the \emph{farm}, not the
animal. Every model below is therefore reported against a control.

\section{Model 1 --- RT-DETRv2, animal and ear-tag detection}

\textbf{Job.} Find the animal in the frame, and find the ear tag. Everything
downstream depends on it: no detection means no pose, no segmentation, and no
measurement.

\subsection*{The version pin that matters more than it looks}

\texttt{ml/requirements.txt} pins \texttt{transformers==5.0.0} exactly, and
\texttt{server/preflight.py} \emph{fails} if anything else is installed.

RT-DETRv2 stores its per-layer prediction heads \textbf{tied}. Version 5.15.1
expects them untied and fills the gap with random weights. The ear-tag path
reads decoder layer~1 --- one of the layers it randomises.

It does not crash. It returns a \bad{confident-looking 0.62 detection with a
box outside the image}, and the value changes on every process start. A silent
wrong answer is far more expensive than a loud failure, which is why the pin is
enforced by a preflight check rather than a comment.

\evidence{\texttt{models/README.md}, \texttt{ml/requirements.txt},
\texttt{server/preflight.py}}

\subsection*{Why decoder layer 1}

The final layer's ear-tag head collapsed to \bad{AP@0.5 = 0.001}. Layer~1 is
read instead. This is recorded in the manifest so that nobody re-discovers it
by debugging a detector that appears to work for animals and not for tags.

\evidence{\texttt{models/MANIFEST.json}}

\section{Model 2 --- HRNet-W32 bovine keypoints}

\textbf{Job.} Place the anatomical landmarks that every conformation
measurement is built from. \textbf{This model is the system's bottleneck, and
the honest ceiling on what the product can score.}

\subsection*{The nineteen joints nobody annotated}

Measured over 40 real photographs, every one of these returns confidence
\bad{0.0\%} --- not low confidence, \emph{zero}, on every image:

\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{udder\_floor}, \texttt{fore\_udder\_top},
\texttt{fore\_udder\_body\_junction}, \texttt{rear\_udder},
\texttt{rear\_udder\_top}, \texttt{rear\_udder\_left},
\texttt{rear\_udder\_right}, \texttt{udder\_cleft\_top},
\texttt{udder\_cleft\_bottom}, \texttt{teat\_front\_left},
\texttt{teat\_front\_right}, \texttt{teat\_front\_left\_top},
\texttt{teat\_front\_left\_bottom}, \texttt{teat\_rear\_left},
\texttt{teat\_rear\_right}, \texttt{teat\_width\_left\_1},
\texttt{teat\_width\_left\_2}, \texttt{vulva\_base}, and the
\texttt{rib\_angle\_*} triple.
\end{quote}

The model was never taught these landmarks exist. \textbf{That blocks 12 of the
20 contract traits outright}, and it is a labelling task, not a modelling one.

\subsection*{What the model does place well}

\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{\hspace{2.2em}}lr@{}}
\toprule
\texttt{chest\_front} & 100\% & \texttt{withers} & 70\% \\
\texttt{hoof\_left/right} & 95\% & \texttt{chest\_bottom} & 60\% \\
\texttt{pastern\_left} & 95\% & \texttt{shoulder\_left} & 55\% \\
\texttt{tail\_head} & 90\% & \texttt{hook\_left} & 38\% \\
\texttt{hock\_left} & 88\% & \bad{\texttt{pin\_right}} & \bad{20\%} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

The pattern is consistent: the model learned the animal's hindquarters and legs
well and its front and rump poorly. That is why leg-angle traits score and
chest and rump traits mostly refuse.

\subsection*{The confidence threshold was measured, not chosen}

\texttt{POSE\_MIN\_KEYPOINT\_CONFIDENCE = 0.10}. Re-gating 55 photographs:

\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Gate} & \textbf{Usable joints} & \textbf{Measurements} & \textbf{In-band rate} \\
\midrule
0.30 & 6.5 & 41 & 34.1\% \\
0.25 & 7.5 & 46 & 39.1\% \\
0.20 & 8.7 & 62 & 35.5\% \\
0.15 & 10.8 & 86 & 37.2\% \\
\textbf{0.10} & \textbf{14.2} & \textbf{142} & \textbf{35.9\%} \\
0.05 & 15.3 & 167 & 34.1\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

The in-band rate is the \emph{control}. If a looser gate were admitting
misplaced joints, the extra measurements would scatter and that rate would fall
toward chance. It does not move --- so the measurements a strict gate was
discarding were as plausible as the ones it kept, and there were three times as
many. 0.05 is where the rate finally slips, so the knee at 0.10 is taken.

\evidence{\texttt{ml/config/models.py}}

\section{Model 3 --- DINOv2 breed and group verification}

\textbf{Job.} Check the photograph against the registered breed. The checkpoint
is 81\,KB because only the linear probes are stored; the DINOv2 backbone is
fetched by \texttt{timm} on first use.

\subsection*{The exact-breed head disables itself}

Measured at \bad{38.1\% source-held-out}. Its confidence carries no usable
signal either: tightening the threshold from answering every time to answering
30\% of the time moves accuracy by only 5.6 points.

The trained checkpoint therefore \textbf{switches its own breed head off}. The
app never names a breed from a photograph, and \texttt{breed\_verified: false}
means \textbf{NOT CHECKED} --- never ``contradicted''. Rendering it as a
mismatch would accuse every correctly registered animal in the district.

\subsection*{The group head, and the control that makes it meaningful}

\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
Group head, source-held-out & \good{80.2\%} \\
Background-only control & 60.7\% \\
Species head, source-held-out & 88.5\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

The background-only control is the important column. It measures what a model
scores when it can see \emph{only the backdrop} and not the animal. A model
that scored 80\% against a 79\% control would have learned the farm. At
80.2\% against 60.7\%, this one has learned something about the animal.

Five groups: \texttt{red\_zebu}, \texttt{grey\_draught},
\texttt{dwarf\_cattle}, \texttt{exotic\_dairy}, \texttt{buffalo}. Where a
group's own recall is poor --- \texttt{exotic\_dairy} at 43\% --- the response
carries \texttt{group\_reliable: false} and the app shows it as a hint rather
than a finding.

\subsection*{Dataset skew}

The Indian Bovine Breeds source has 36--439 images per class across 41
breed folders. That imbalance is the reason the exact-breed head cannot be
trusted and the coarser group head can.

\section{Model 4 --- SAM2 hiera-tiny}

\textbf{Not trained by this team.} Meta's published checkpoint, Apache-2.0,
used as-is for silhouette segmentation.

It matters more than its size suggests. The 2D-to-3D weight estimator, the
\texttt{chest\_bottom} landmark and the udder floor all depend on a real
silhouette. SAM2 was once \emph{absent from the server environment entirely},
and \texttt{segment\_animal} silently fell back to the detection box as a plain
rectangle on every image --- disabling all three without a single error. It is
now pinned in the requirements and checked at preflight.

\evidence{\texttt{STATUS-measured.md}, \texttt{ml/detection/detector.py}}

\textbf{Status: not in the shipped system.} No checkpoint is in this
repository and none is on any branch. This is why the app reports
\textbf{NOT SCREENED} rather than a clean bill of health --- an empty symptom
list is not a clinical finding.

\subsection*{Why it is not integrated}

Two blockers, neither about the model itself.

\textbf{The source-held-out figure may be measuring an untrained head.} Each
symptom's labels live entirely in one source --- Mendeley carries
\texttt{skin\_nodules} and masks circular patches, Roboflow the reverse. Holding
out a source therefore removes \emph{all} supervision for that head. A true
source-held-out experiment may not be possible on this dataset as built, and
the fix would be skin-nodule images from a second source.

\textbf{The background-only control did not complete.} It returned empty
metrics after RT-DETR checkpoint warnings. A run that errored and predicted
nothing yields the same 0.000 recall as a model that correctly refused, so it
cannot be read as the control passing.

By this project's own standard --- the exact-breed head disables itself at
38.1\% --- \texttt{skin\_nodules} at 0.390 should also disable itself.
\texttt{circular\_skin\_patches} at 0.889 sits nearer the group head's 80.2\%,
but rests on only 9 positive test examples (Wilson 95\% CI 0.57--0.98).

\section{What this means for the product}

The trait ceiling is not a bug and not a tuning problem:

\begin{tabular}{@{}lrp{7.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Traits} & \textbf{Count} & \textbf{Blocked by} \\
\midrule
Blocked & 12 & Udder, teat and rib landmarks never annotated \\
Weak & 3 & \texttt{pin\_right} resolves 20\% of the time \\
Reachable & 5 & Of which 5 need a centimetre scale from the ear tag \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

So the honest ceiling today is \textbf{5 to 8 traits}, and the scorecard states
which ones and why each of the rest refused. Reaching 20 requires an annotation
session, not a better model.
