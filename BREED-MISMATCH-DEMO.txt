BREED MISMATCH DEMO - animal 356279812354 (Hariana, cattle)
===========================================================

A bonus demo that needs no traits at all.


WHAT IT IS
----------
A real matched pair - the SAME Holstein cow, side view and rear view, taken
from consecutive frames of one video as she walks past the camera and then
away from it. This is the only genuinely matched side+rear pair found in any
dataset on this machine.

Everywhere else in this pack, the rear photograph is a different animal from
the side photograph, because the breed datasets hold one photograph per animal
and have no concept of the same cow from two angles. Here they really are the
same cow.


WHAT TO DO
----------
Load side.jpg, rear.jpg and tag.jpg, and type animal id 356279812354.

That record says Hariana - an Indian breed. The photograph is a Holstein.


WHAT HAPPENS
------------
  engine             ml-pipeline
  predicted_species  cattle
  predicted_group    exotic_dairy
  group_consistent   FALSE            <- the point

The app renders that as a warning on the breed identity card: the photograph
does NOT match the registered breed, worth checking the record.

Note what it does NOT do: it never corrects the record automatically. From
here, a records error and a photograph error look identical, and only a person
can tell them apart.

Only 1 trait scores (Rear Legs Set, 141.8 degrees). These are 640x480 video
frames - too small for reliable landmarks. That is the trade in these
datasets: you can have a matched pair, or enough resolution to measure, but
not both. Gir_107.jpg, by comparison, is 5184x3456.


WHY IT IS WORTH SHOWING
-----------------------
Two reasons.

It demonstrates the breed check working on a real mismatch, rather than being
described. And it is the one set in this pack where every number describes the
same animal, because the side and the rear genuinely are the same cow.

If a judge has already asked why the rear photographs elsewhere are a
different animal, this is the honest answer to follow it with: "here is one
where they aren't - and you can see what it costs us in resolution."
