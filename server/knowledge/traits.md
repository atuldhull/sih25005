# The 20 NDDB type traits

## Stature
Dairy Strength trait, measured from the side photo in centimetres: the height
from the ground to the top of the withers (the ridge between the shoulder
blades). Tall vs short is a breeding preference, not quality.

## Heart Girth
Dairy Strength trait: the circumference of the chest just behind the front
legs. A girth is a distance AROUND the body, so it cannot be read from a flat
photo directly - the system reconstructs it from the 3D body model, or
estimates it from body depth. It also drives the weight estimate.

## Body Length
Dairy Strength trait, side view, centimetres: from the point of the shoulder
to the pin bone at the rear. Together with heart girth it feeds the weight
formula.

## Body Depth
Dairy Strength trait, side view, centimetres: the depth of the barrel
measured at the last rib - how deep the body is from the top line down to the
belly floor.

## Angularity
Dairy Strength trait, side view, an angle: how open and sloped the rib
structure appears. Angular, open ribbing is associated with dairy character.
Angles need no ruler, so this trait works even without the ear-tag scale.

## Rump Angle
Rump trait, side view, an angle: the slope of the line from the hook bone
(hip) to the pin bone. Score 5 is roughly a moderate slope; the extremes are
pins clearly higher or clearly lower than hooks.

## Rump Width
Rump trait, rear view, centimetres: the distance between the pin bones. A
wider rump is associated with calving ease. For buffalo the reference
landmarks differ slightly from cattle.

## Rear Legs Set
Feet & Legs trait, side view, an angle: the angle at the hock joint. Very
straight (posty) and very curved (sickle) are the two extremes. NDDB scores
this subjectively, so wider uncertainty is normal.

## Rear Legs Rear View
Feet & Legs trait, judged from the walking video: how straight the rear legs
track when seen from behind. Hocks turning inward (cow-hocked) is one
extreme. Movement shows this better than any still photo.

## Foot Angle
Feet & Legs trait, side view, an angle: the angle of the front hoof wall
against the ground. Low, flat angles and very steep angles are the extremes.
An officially subjective trait.

## Fore Udder Attachment
Udder trait, side view: how strongly and smoothly the front of the udder
blends into the body wall. Loose attachment is one extreme, very tight the
other. Subjective trait, judged relative to the body - needs no ruler.

## Rear Udder Height
Udder trait, rear view, centimetres: the distance from the base of the vulva
down to where the milk-secreting tissue begins. Higher rear udders are
associated with capacity over a long life.

## Central Ligament
Udder trait, rear view: the depth of the cleft between the left and right
udder halves. The ligament is the udder's internal support - a clear cleft
means strong support. Subjective trait.

## Udder Depth
Udder trait, rear view: the position of the udder floor relative to the hock.
An udder carried well above the hock is one extreme; below the hock is the
other. Timing matters: a full udder measures differently, which is why the
guideline says to photograph before evening milking.

## Front Teat Placement
Udder trait, rear view: where the front teats sit on their quarters -
pointing outward, centred, or inward. Officially subjective; scored relative
to the quarter, so no ruler is needed.

## Teat Length
Udder trait, rear view, centimetres. Measured on the LEFT FRONT teat for
cattle but on the LEFT REAR teat for buffalo - one of exactly two documented
differences between the species in the scorecard.

## Rear Teat Placement
Udder trait, rear view: where the rear teats sit on their quarters.
Officially subjective, ratio-based, no ruler needed.

## Rear Udder Width
Udder trait, rear view, centimetres: the width of the rear udder attachment.
Wider attachment is associated with capacity.

## Teat Thickness
Udder trait, rear view, centimetres: the diameter of the teat. Like Teat
Length, measured on the left front teat for cattle and the left rear teat
for buffalo.

## Body Condition Score
General trait: the fat cover of the animal, assessed from the shape of the
pelvic and tailhead region - vets normally judge it by touch, and the system
reads the same area's surface shape. 1 is emaciated, 9 is very fat; for a
milking cow the desirable zone is in the middle and depends on her stage of
lactation.
