# Buffalo differences and weight estimation

## How buffalo scoring differs from cattle
The same 20-trait scorecard covers both species, with exactly two documented
differences: the rump-width reference landmarks are illustrated differently
for buffalo, and teat length and thickness are measured on the LEFT REAR teat
in buffalo instead of the left front teat in cattle. Horn shape, coat colour
and dewlap belong to breed identification and appear nowhere in the type
scorecard.

## How weight is estimated without a weighbridge
The app uses the veterinary girth-length method: heart girth squared,
multiplied by body length, divided by a calibration constant. Because both
measurements carry some uncertainty, weight is always reported as a range
(for example 385-412 kg), never as a single exact number. A 3D body-volume
estimate cross-checks the result when available.

## Why weight matters in the record
The weight trend across sessions tells the farmer whether feeding is working
and helps the vet spot problems early - a falling trend in a milking animal
is worth a check-up even when nothing looks wrong.
