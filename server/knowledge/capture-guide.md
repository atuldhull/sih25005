# Capture guide - how to photograph the animal correctly

## How to take the side photo
Stand about 3-4 meters from the animal, directly from the side (90
degrees to the body), with the camera at the height of the animal's
belly. The WHOLE animal must be in the frame - nose to tail, hooves to
top of the shoulder - with some space around it. The animal should
stand square on level ground with its head up in a natural position.
Side-view traits like Stature, Body Length, Body Depth, Angularity,
Rump Angle and Rear Legs Set are all measured from this photo, so a
tilted or cut-off side photo lowers their confidence or makes them
unscorable.

## How to take the rear photo
Stand 2-3 meters directly behind the animal with the camera at udder
height. Both hind legs, the full udder and both hip bones must be
visible. The tail should hang naturally - if it hides the udder, wait
or gently move it aside. Rear-view traits like Rump Width, Rear Udder
Height, Rear Udder Width, Central Ligament, teat traits and Udder
Depth come from this photo. In buffaloes the rear teats matter
especially, so make sure the teats are clearly visible.

## The walking video
Record about 8 seconds of the animal walking in a straight line away
from or past the camera, from behind. Keep the phone steady. The
video is used for Rear Legs Rear View and for gait screening (a limp
shows as an uneven walking rhythm). If the animal will not walk, the
app can still score the photo traits - the video trait is simply
marked "not scored" with the reason.

## The ear tag must be visible
The animal's official ear tag is used as the measuring ruler: its
printed parts have a known size in millimeters, so when the tag is
visible in the photo the app can convert pixels into centimeters. In
the side photo, make sure the tagged ear is on the camera side and
the tag face is readable. If the tag is hidden, dirty or turned away,
centimeter traits (like Stature or Body Length in cm) fall back to
"not measured" and only angle and ratio traits are scored.

## Common capture mistakes
1. Animal cut off at the edges - always leave space around the body.
2. Photo taken from the front at an angle - measurements get
   distorted; stand at a true side or true rear position.
3. Camera too high (shooting downward) - keep it level at belly
   height.
4. Strong shadow or backlight - stand so the sun is behind YOU.
5. Animal standing in mud or slope - hooves must be visible on
   roughly level ground.
6. Ear tag hidden - the app loses its ruler and cm measurements are
   refused rather than guessed.
A bad capture is never silently accepted: the trait is marked "not
scored" with the reason, and retaking the photo fixes it.
